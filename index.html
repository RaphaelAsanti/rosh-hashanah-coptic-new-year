Calendar Concurrence — Rosh Hashanah and Coptic New Year

This page documents a calendar concurrence occurring this year: Rosh Hashanah in the Jewish calendar and Coptic New Year (Nayrouz) in the Coptic Christian calendar fall on the same civil date.

Rosh Hashanah — Jewish Judgment Day

Rosh Hashanah marks the beginning of the Jewish year and carries the theme of judgment:

  • the opening of the heavenly books
  • the accounting of deeds
  • the establishment of moral order
  • the start of the Ten Days of Repentance

In Jewish liturgy, it is the day when humanity stands before God for evaluation.

Coptic New Year — Christian Resurrection Day Symbolism

Coptic New Year (1 Thout), also called Nayrouz, marks the beginning of the Coptic liturgical year. Its symbolism centers on resurrection:

  • renewal and rebirth
  • the triumph of life over death
  • the commemoration of martyrs
  • the start of a new spiritual cycle

In Coptic tradition, the new year is a celebration of the victory of resurrection.

How This Concurrence Relates to the Nun–Shin Structure

The ebook discusses a structural pairing between two symbolic elements: nun (life, seed, renewal) and shin (fire, exposure, judgment).

This concurrence mirrors that structure:

  • Nayrouz → nun: resurrection, renewal, the seed of life.
  • Rosh Hashanah → shin: judgment, exposure, the sorting of deeds.

This page records that resurrection symbolism and judgment symbolism occur on the same civil date this year. No prediction or doctrinal claim is made. It is a structural observation relevant to the thematic architecture of the ebook.
