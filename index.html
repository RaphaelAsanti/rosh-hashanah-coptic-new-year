Personal Note — Calendar Concurrence

This note records a simple calendrical fact: Rosh Hashanah and Coptic New Year (Nayrouz) fall on the same civil date this year.

Rosh Hashanah is the Jewish Judgment Day. Coptic New Year is the Christian Resurrection Day.

How This Concurrence Relates to the Nun–Shin Structure

In the ebook, nun and shin are defined as two linked actions:

  • nun — the fish: the act of separating the good fish from the bad fish.
  • shin — the fire: the burning, the judgment, the exposure of what remains.

Normally these two actions are sequential — first the sorting (nun), then the fire (shin).

But this year’s concurrence does something different: Resurrection Day (nun) was moved forward into the same civil date as Judgment Day (shin).

This means the fish‑sorting and the fire occur together, symbolically:

  • Nayrouz → nun: the act of selecting what lives.
  • Rosh Hashanah → shin: the act of judging by fire.
  • This year → both at once: the nun–shin of burning and judging simultaneously.

This page records that specific structural alignment. It makes no prediction and no doctrinal claim. It simply notes that the theme described in the ebook — fish and fire united in one moment — appears symbolically in the calendar this year.
